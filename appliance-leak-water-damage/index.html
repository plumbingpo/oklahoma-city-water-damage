Dealing With Appliance Leak Water Damage in Oklahoma City

Most of the appliance leaks we see trace back to three machines. When the supply hose on a washing machine splits, water flows under full pressure until somebody turns it off. With a dishwasher the leak is typically slow, coming from the door seal or the drain hose where the cabinets hide it. The ice maker line on a refrigerator is a thin tube, and it can drip for months behind the unit. No two of these call for the same cleanup.

On every Oklahoma City job, the first step is moving the appliance and tracing the path the water took. For a kitchen, the usual places are below the flooring and under the cabinets. Laundry room water goes into the wall at the back of the machine and the neighboring rooms, and with an upstairs laundry, the ceiling underneath. Standing water is extracted, absorbed moisture is measured, and drying equipment is sized to fit the wet area.

Before We Arrive

  1. Shut the Machine DownIf the outlet and the floor next to it are both dry, end the cycle and take the plug out of the wall. If not, switch it off at the breaker instead.
  2. Shut Off the Water LineFor a washing machine, close both the cold and hot valves on the wall at its back. The dishwasher valve is under the kitchen sink. A refrigerator line has a small valve below or behind the unit.
  3. Mop Up What You CanGetting reachable water up with a mop or towels stops it from moving under the cabinets and the floor. Do not vacuum unless the machine is rated for wet and dry use.
  4. Leave the Door ShutA dishwasher or washer that still holds water should stay closed. The entire load of water may spill across the floor if the door is opened mid-cycle.
  5. Photograph the Failed PartTake pictures of the hose, valve or fitting that failed and keep the part. Your insurer and the appliance maker may both ask to see it.
Our Process

How We Handle Appliance Leak Water Damage

The appliance is disconnected and moved, and the supply is confirmed off. Then thermal imaging and moisture meters show us where water went below flooring, under the cabinets and into the walls nearby.

We extract standing water out of carpet and off hard floors. If it is trapped beneath a floating floor or the cabinets, toe kicks come off or boards are lifted so we can get to it.

Coverage

What About Insurance?

When a machine overflows unexpectedly or a hose bursts, that is typically treated as sudden and accidental, which many homeowners policies cover. What is typically paid is the water damage, while the failed part and the appliance are left out. Gradual leaks usually fall outside coverage, so slow seepage at a dishwasher or an ice maker line makes for a tougher claim. Some insurers also ask about the age and upkeep of the hose. Before cleanup, take photos, save the failed part and read your policy.

What It Costs

What Drives the Price

  • Removal of cabinets or flooring, compared with drying them in place when that is possible.
  • Which appliance leaked and whether it was a fast flood or a slow seep.
  • Presence of mold, which adds removal work in front of the drying.
  • How many rooms the water reached, including any ceiling below an upstairs laundry.

The Usual Culprits

How It Usually Starts

  • Poor installation, such as a dishwasher set out of level or a supply fitting not fully tightened.
  • Aging rubber supply hoses on a washing machine, which weaken and then split under pressure.
  • Inlet valves that stick open, so the dishwasher or washer keeps taking in water past the fill level.
  • A clogged defrost drain or cracked drain pan that lets a refrigerator leak onto the floor.
  • Worn dishwasher door gaskets, cracked drain hoses or a failed pump seal.
If It Is Left

What Happens if You Wait

Kitchen Cabinets

Toe kicks and cabinet sides take on water from underneath when a dishwasher leaks. Panels swell and come apart, and a wet cabinet box can keep the wall behind it damp for weeks.

Rooms Next Door

For hours, a failed washer hose can keep releasing water. Hallways, bedrooms and closets get wet as it travels under the walls, and carpet and pad are soaked a good distance from the laundry.

Hidden Mold at the Back

Behind a refrigerator or a dishwasher it is warm and dark, and nobody checks there often. A slow drip there feeds mold on the wall and floor long before anyone notices.

Downstairs Ceiling Damage

The ceiling under an upper floor laundry catches whatever water makes it past the floor above. Sagging, staining and soaked insulation above the drywall are the usual result.
