How to Prevent Storm Damage Repair Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Keep gutters and downspouts clear so water drains properly in heavy rain
  • Learn where the main water shutoff is and how to close it if pipes are damaged in a storm
  • Cracked caulk lets blown rain in, so inspect and renew the seals on doors and windows
  • Ice dams form when attics run warm, so check that yours has enough insulation and airflow
  • Schedule a roof inspection every year so weak spots are repaired before storm season
  • Cut back limbs that hang over or close to the house so wind cannot throw them into it

Warning Signs for Storm Damage Repair

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • After a weather event, insulation inside walls or overhead feels soaked
  • From ground level, the roof shows shingles torn off, broken or raised
  • Metal flashing pulled away from vents, valleys or the edge of the roof
  • A basement or ground floor that floods when the drains cannot keep up
  • Wind-driven rain coming in through windows, doors or wall penetrations
  • Downspouts knocked off and gutter runs hanging after high wind
  • Water stains that show up on ceilings or walls during or after a storm

The Benefits of Professional Storm Damage Repair

The repairs a storm leaves behind are tangled together, and sorting them out correctly calls for an experienced restoration crew. What seems like a small leak indoors can hide widespread water damage in wall cavities, roof decking and insulation, which turns into mold and structural decay unless it is identified and dried properly. Professional restoration technicians use thermal imaging and moisture meters to trace water paths and identify every affected area.

Emergency board-up and tarping also take proper technique to be effective. Tarps put on the wrong way may funnel water into the building or tear loose in the wind.
